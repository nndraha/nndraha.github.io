### The Quick Summary

*Salmonella* is a very important germ that can make chickens sick and is bad for our food safety, costing a lot of money. Because of this, we need a good way to find out if chickens have been infected with *Salmonella*. This study made a new, special test called an indirect ELISA. This test uses a part of the *Salmonella* germ, called SptP protein, to find tiny fighters (antibodies) in chicken blood. These fighters show if the chicken has been exposed to *Salmonella*. The new SptP-ELISA test works very well; it is good at finding only *Salmonella* and is very accurate. This helps us keep our chickens and our food safe from this germ.

### Practical Implications

This study's development of an SptP-ELISA offers a significant practical implication for food science by enhancing the ability to detect *Salmonella* in poultry. Early and accurate detection allows for more effective surveillance and control measures on farms, preventing infected birds from entering the food supply chain. By reducing the presence of *Salmonella* at the source, the risk of foodborne illnesses in humans is substantially lowered. This diagnostic tool also contributes to the economic stability of the poultry industry by minimizing product recalls, reducing losses from outbreaks, and fostering consumer confidence in poultry products. Ultimately, it strengthens public health efforts by providing a crucial tool for *Salmonella* prevention and management within the food system.

### Potential Use in Indonesia

In Indonesia, where poultry is a staple and often sold through traditional markets and street food vendors, a reliable diagnostic tool for *Salmonella* is crucial. This SptP-ELISA could enable faster monitoring of poultry health at the farm level, reducing contamination risks before products reach diverse local supply chains. This is particularly vital in a tropical climate, where the rapid multiplication of pathogens like *Salmonella* poses a constant challenge to food safety.
